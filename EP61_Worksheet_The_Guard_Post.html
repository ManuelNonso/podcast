Before You Begin

How to Use This Worksheet

This is the coaching session the episode prepared you for, in written form, built to actually change an order you obey before this week is over, not just how you feel today. Four parts. Nine exercises. A full trace of exactly who built the post you're guarding, and one complete practice for deciding, consciously, whether it still deserves the soldier.

This worksheet is not about army barracks or beef. It is about the actual order running quietly in your life right now, in your family, your money, your body, your career, or how much of yourself you let show up in a room, and the actual moment someone gave it to you, whether or not they meant to.

Give yourself real time with Exercise 2 especially. Most self-help treats a limiting belief as something to simply "think positive" about. This worksheet treats it as an actual order, given by an actual person, at an actual moment, for an actual reason, one you can trace and therefore one you can finally re-examine on your own terms.

Not every order deserves to be cancelled. Some guard posts are still protecting something real. This worksheet does not ask you to tear down every rule you've ever inherited. It asks you to finally walk out and check, one domain at a time, honestly.

🪖 The Guard Post — Hold This Throughout

Years ago, someone poured cement slabs on a patch of army ground. Goats kept wandering over and damaging them before they cured. A commander made a reasonable call: post a soldier there until the job was done. Then he was transferred. The project was never finished. The reason disappeared. Nobody ever cancelled the order, so every commander after him kept sending soldiers to guard nothing, assuming there must still be a reason.

This worksheet is how you walk out to your own guard post, in every part of your life where an order has been running longer than its reason has been true, and finally decide, on purpose, whether the goat is still there.

What This Session Will Produce
  • Something named — the specific order you've been obeying, and the exact sentence it would sound like if it were spoken out loud, in the area of life costing you the most right now
  • Something traced — the person, the moment, and the real problem they were actually solving when the order was first given
  • Something tested — a clear, honest verdict on whether the original reason is still true today, or quietly expired years ago
  • Something committed — one complete Ask Why Practice, tested against real pushback, and practiced starting this week
Part One

Discover — Naming the Order, Tracing Who Gave It

Before you can decide whether an order still deserves your obedience, you need to see clearly which order is currently running your life, who actually gave it to you, and what they were really protecting at the time.

1
The Domain Scan
Naming the order currently running, unexamined, in every major area of your life.

Most people can name that they feel "stuck" or "limited" in general. Far fewer can name, precisely, the actual sentence they've been obeying, in each specific area of their life, without ever having tested it.

Coaching Prompt

For each domain below, name the guard post, the specific rule, limit, or "I just don't do that" currently running unexamined, and what it's costing you to keep obeying it without question.

Be precise. Not "I have money issues," but the actual order, a specific belief about what you're allowed to earn, ask for, or keep, stated the way it would sound out loud.

2
The Root — Tracing Who Built This Post, and Why
A four part root-cause trace. Take your time here.

Choose the one domain from Exercise 1 costing you the most right now. This is the one you'll work through for the rest of this worksheet.

2a — Who Gave the Order
Coaching Prompt

Who is the person, real or institutional, who first handed you this rule? A parent, a grandparent, an early boss, a teacher, a painful season in your own life that generalized itself into a permanent policy?

See them clearly, not as a villain, but as the commander who first posted the soldier, doing the best they could with the information and the power they had, in a moment that has since passed.

2b — What They Were Actually Protecting
Coaching Prompt

What was the real, understandable problem this person was solving when they gave you this order? What were the goats, the actual risk or fear that made the order reasonable, at the time?

This is not about excusing harm. It is about seeing the decision clearly enough to know exactly what it was actually built to guard against.

2c — The Exact Sentence
Coaching Prompt

If this order were compressed into a single sentence, the rule you've been unconsciously obeying, what would that sentence be?

Write it exactly as it would sound out loud. It may never have been said to you directly. It may simply be the rule you wrote for yourself after watching or living through what you named above.

2d — The Cost Tally
Coaching Prompt

What has obeying this order actually cost you, in this specific domain, across the time you've carried it?

Include missed opportunities, relationships held back, energy spent guarding instead of building, or a version of yourself that never got to show up, because the old order said not to.

Notice ThisThe order you just named was very likely a reasonable call once, made by someone reacting to something real, with the information and power they had at the time. Naming it precisely is not an act of blame toward them, or toward yourself for obeying it this long. It's the first honest step toward finally deciding whether it still belongs.

3
The Diagnosis — Is the Goat Still There?
Naming plainly whether the original reason for this order is still real, or already expired.

This diagnosis determines which path you'll build in Part Two, and getting it right changes everything about what you do next.

Coaching Prompt

Look honestly at what you named in 2b, what this order was actually protecting against. Is that reason still true, today, in your actual life? Or has it already been gone for years, quietly, while you kept obeying the order anyway?

Some orders are still protecting something real. This is not about rejecting every rule you've ever been given. It's about finally checking, one at a time, honestly.

"The soldier wasn't wrong to be posted there, the first time. He was wrong to still be standing there years after the job was finished, because nobody ever told him he could leave. Nobody's told you either. I'm telling you now."

5
The Words — Drafting Your Ask Why Question
Preparing the exact question, for someone else or for yourself, before you need it.

The daughter in the beef story only broke her pattern the moment she finally said one question out loud, to someone who was still alive to answer it. Some of your orders, you can still trace back to a living source. Others, you'll have to trace using your own honest memory. Either way, the question has to actually get asked, out loud or on paper, not just thought about.

Coaching Prompt

If you could ask one direct question, either to the person who gave you this order, or to yourself, about why it's still running, what would that question be?

Keep it simple and specific enough to actually ask this week, not a vague "why am I like this," but the exact words you'd use.

Say it out loud once before moving on. If the person you'd ask is still alive and safe to ask, put a real date next to when you'll actually ask them. If not, this question becomes the one you sit with honestly, in writing, in Part Three.

Part Three

Pressure Test — Testing Your Practice Against Real Life

An order is easy to question in a quiet moment alone with a worksheet. It's harder to question when someone defends it, when old guilt resurfaces, or when there's nobody left to ask. Unless you've already thought it through.

6
Scenario One — Someone Defends the Old Order
When the person who gave you the order, or someone who also obeys it, pushes back.
The Scenario

You gently question the order, out loud, to the person who gave it to you or to someone else who has also been obeying it their whole life. They get defensive, or say some version of "that's just how it's always been done," without actually answering why.

This scenario tests whether you can hold your own honest verdict without needing their agreement to act on it.
7
Scenario Two — The Old Guilt Resurfaces
When you finally break the pattern, and the exact inherited belief returns.
The Scenario

You've done the one thing this week that the old order would never have allowed. Then the exact sentence from Exercise 2c resurfaces, telling you that you've done something careless, disloyal, or dangerous by finally putting the order down.

This scenario tests whether you can recognize the old order in a new outfit, rather than obeying it automatically again.
8
Scenario Three — There's Nobody Left to Ask
When the source of the order is gone, and you can't ask them directly.
The Scenario

Unlike the grandmother in the story, the person who handed you this order isn't alive, or isn't safe or possible to ask directly. You have to trace the reason using only your own honest memory and best reconstruction.

This scenario tests whether you can act on your own honest best judgment, without a living witness to confirm it.
Part Four

Protect — Making This a Practice, Not a Moment

One order retired is a good start. It is not the whole shift. Part Four turns this worksheet into an ongoing practice, so a new unexamined order doesn't quietly take root somewhere else before you notice.

9
The Weekly Practice — Building the Habit
The simple, recurring practice that catches new guard posts early, plus a relapse plan.

The goal is not one retired order. The goal is building the lifelong habit of noticing quickly when you're obeying a rule you've never actually questioned, in whatever domain it shows up next.

  • Daily: Before you default to the old order, say your Ask Why question from Exercise 5 out loud once.
  • Within 7 Days: Take the specific action from your Exercise 4 branch, retirement or conscious keep, with the accountability mechanism you named.
  • Every Week: Ask yourself one question. "What did I do this week purely out of habit, without checking if the reason was still real?"
  • Every Month: Revisit your Domain Scan from Exercise 1. Has a new unquestioned order started running somewhere else?
  • Whenever an Old Order Resurfaces: Use that exact moment as proof the system is working, not evidence that you failed. Noticing the guard post is the win.

Before You Close This

A soldier stood guard over cement that had finished curing years earlier, because nobody ever told him he could leave. A family threw away good meat for two generations, for a fridge that was eventually bought. Neither pattern broke because someone tried harder. Both broke the moment someone finally asked one honest question.

Roger Bannister didn't break the four-minute mile because his body suddenly changed. He broke it because he tested a belief nobody had actually tested. Forty-six days later, John Landy did the same, because the order that it couldn't be done had just been proven false, in public, by someone willing to check.

Living in Full Expression means building the practiced habit of walking out to your own guard posts, again and again, especially in the exact moments it feels safer to just keep obeying without asking.

Before you close this session — write the one thing you now know, that you did not know when you opened it.

The order will not cancel itself. You now know exactly who gave it, exactly what it was protecting, and exactly whether the reason is still real. The only thing left is walking out to check.
